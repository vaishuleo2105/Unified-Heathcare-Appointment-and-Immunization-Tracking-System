P40 [normal]: SYNOPSIS
P41 [normal]: Unified Healthcare Appointment and Immunization Tracking System is a web-based comprehensive healthcare management platform designed to eliminate record fragmentation across hospitals, streamline appointment scheduling, manage maternal and child immunization schedules, and optimize clinical doctor workloads. Patient medical information often remains isolated within individual hospital databases, leading to delayed emergency treatment, redundant diagnostic tests, and untracked vaccination milestones. The system consolidates medical histories into a unified record accessible to authorized healthcare providers across participating institutions.
P42 [normal]: The system supports Patient, Doctor, Hospital Staff, and Hospital Administrator workflows. Built on the MERN stack (MongoDB, Express.js, React.js, Node.js) and integrated with a Python Flask Machine Learning microservice, it manages patient registration, digital ABHA ID authentication, online appointment booking, unified medical history tracking, inter-hospital emergency referrals, government maternal ID (RCH/PICME) tracking, and multi-language accessibility across six regional languages (English, Hindi, Tamil, Telugu, Kannada, Malayalam). Machine learning algorithms, specifically Gradient Boosting Classifiers, are implemented to predict appointment urgency types (Emergency, Consultation, Follow-up, Vaccination, General Checkup) and calculate doctor workload risk scores (0-100) for effective clinical schedule balancing.
P43 [normal]: The application utilizes React.js with TailwindCSS for a responsive client interface, Node.js and Express.js for core REST API services, MongoDB with Mongoose for document storage, node-cron for automated background reminder scheduling, and Python Flask for machine learning inference. Digital health identity is integrated using standard ABHA (Ayushman Bharat Health Account) Sandbox API specifications. Inter-hospital referral features allow instant transfer of emergency medical summaries, current medications, allergies, and diagnoses during patient transfers. An automated background reminder scheduler runs continuously to notify patients of upcoming appointments, follow-ups, medication schedules, and child vaccination due dates.
P44 [normal]: The main objective of the system is to provide an integrated, intelligent environment for healthcare administration and clinical record continuity. By combining electronic health records, predictive machine learning models, ABHA digital health identities, maternal-child health tracking, automated reminder schedulers, and robust API services, the platform establishes a reliable foundation for modern healthcare management.

P73 [normal]: 1.1 PROJECT OVERVIEW
P74 [normal]: Healthcare delivery systems depend heavily on continuous patient information access, timely clinical decision-making, and proactive preventive care. Patients frequently consult multiple healthcare providers across different hospitals. When medical histories are maintained in isolated hospital databases, doctors lack access to previous diagnoses, prescriptions, lab results, and known allergies.
P75 [normal]: The Unified Healthcare Appointment and Immunization Tracking System is a web-based comprehensive healthcare platform proposed to address these challenges. It provides a centralized environment for managing patient appointments, consolidating electronic health records across hospitals, authenticating national ABHA (Ayushman Bharat Health Account) health IDs, tracking maternal and child immunization schedules using government-issued registration IDs (RCH/PICME), delivering multi-language UI accessibility, and balancing clinical doctor workloads using machine learning.
P76 [normal]: The system incorporates clinical patient datasets to train predictive models. Machine learning algorithms, including Gradient Boosting Classifiers and Random Forest models, are trained to perform two main tasks: predicting appointment urgency types (Emergency, Consultation, Follow-up, Vaccination, General Checkup) based on patient clinical parameters, and computing clinical workload risk scores (0-100) to assist in balancing doctor consultation schedules.
P77 [normal]: The platform supports four principal user roles: Patient, Doctor, Hospital Staff, and Hospital Administrator. Patients can search for doctors, book appointment slots, view their unified medical history, track vaccination due dates, link ABHA health IDs, select their preferred language, and receive automated reminders. Doctors can manage consultation slots, view complete patient histories, record diagnoses and prescriptions, initiate inter-hospital emergency referrals, and review their workload risk scores. Hospital Staff assist in patient onboarding, slot management, and vaccination logging. Hospital Administrators oversee doctor onboarding, department configuration, and overall system operations.
P78 [normal]: The application integrates React.js for the responsive frontend user interface, Node.js and Express.js for backend RESTful APIs, node-cron for automated background reminder scheduling, MongoDB for flexible document database storage, Python Flask for machine learning inference, and scikit-learn for model training. The system is designed as a production-ready web application providing a scalable solution for modern healthcare delivery.

P86 [normal]: 1.3 SCOPE OF THE PROJECT

Furthermore, the scope integrates a Python Flask Machine Learning microservice executing trained Gradient Boosting Classifiers to predict appointment urgency triage types and compute clinical doctor workload risk scores (0–100) for schedule optimization. All data transactions are secured using JSON Web Tokens (JWT), bcryptjs password hashing, and role-based access control.

P94 [normal]: 2.1 EXISTING SYSTEM
P95 [normal]: In traditional healthcare delivery, patient records are maintained independently by each hospital or clinic in isolated electronic systems or paper files. When a patient visits a new hospital or is transferred during a medical emergency, doctors cannot access previous diagnoses, active prescriptions, lab results, or known allergies. National digital health identification such as ABHA ID is often absent, preventing record interoperability.
P96 [normal]: Maternal care and child immunization tracking are often handled through physical paper cards or isolated regional registers. Mothers frequently miss antenatal checkups or vaccination due dates due to lack of automated background reminders. Language barriers in rural areas further hinder patient interaction. Furthermore, appointment scheduling relies on manual queuing, and doctor consultation workloads are assigned without considering patient clinical severity.
P97 [normal]: The existing approach represents a fragmented healthcare environment in which medical history continuity, emergency referral data sharing, digital health identity, immunization tracking, and doctor workload management operate in silos without an integrated technological framework.
P98 [normal]: 2.2 LIMITATIONS OF EXISTING SYSTEM
P99 [normal]: Fragmented health records – medical histories are isolated within individual hospital databases.
P100 [normal]: Lack of digital health identity – absence of ABHA ID integration prevents seamless cross-hospital patient identification.
P101 [normal]: Delayed emergency care – emergency physicians lack immediate access to transferred patients' medical histories and allergies during referrals.
P102 [normal]: Untracked immunizations and missed checkups – physical paper cards and lack of automated cron reminders lead to missed vaccination doses.
P103 [normal]: Redundant diagnostic testing – doctors order duplicate lab tests due to missing historical medical records.
P104 [normal]: Language accessibility barriers – interfaces lack regional language support for rural patients.
P105 [normal]: Manual appointment triage – appointments are scheduled without evaluating clinical urgency or patient risk.
P106 [normal]: Unbalanced doctor workloads – consultation schedules do not account for cumulative patient severity.
